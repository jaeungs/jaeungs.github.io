Teaching Interests

My teaching expertise is data analytics for business, covering data management and SQL, programming for data science, machine learning, text mining, time series forecasting, and econometrics for causal inference. Since 2022, I have taught three courses in the MS in Business Analytics and Project Management (MSBAPM) program at the University of Connecticut. Before joining UConn, I taught database management to undergraduates at KoreaTech and served as a teaching assistant for MBA and master's analytics courses at KAIST between 2016 and 2022. My teaching rests on three commitments: students should understand how a method works and what it assumes, apply it to real business problems, and judge whether a conclusion is supported by the evidence and whether acting on it is responsible. I bring cases from industry and from my own research into class, and I integrate generative AI as a tool that extends what students can do. I am also interested in developing and teaching new courses, including (but not limited to) AI Governance and Ethics, Data Science for Social Good, and Machine Learning for Causal Inference.

Courses Taught

University of Connecticut

[Master's] OPIM 5671: Data Mining and Time Series Forecasting
  • Fall 2022, Spring 2023, Fall 2023, Spring 2024, Fall 2024, Spring 2025, Fall 2025, Spring 2026, Fall 2026
  • This course discusses data mining techniques that can be utilized to analyze large-scale operational data and extract actionable information and knowledge (meaningful patterns, trends, and anomalies) to help make decisions. It is organized around two themes: (1) time series and panel data analysis, from ARIMA models and time series with regressors and events to causal inference with panel data; and (2) text mining and machine learning, from text preprocessing to recurrent neural networks and transformer-based models, together with the ethical challenges of machine learning such as fairness and privacy. These methods can be applied to various business domains, such as digital marketing, supply chain management, corporate finance, and asset pricing. Students implement all methods in Python via Google Colab.
[Master's] OPIM 5272: Data Management and Business Process Modeling
  • Spring 2026
  • This course introduces the principles and practices of data management and business process modeling, with a strong emphasis on relational databases and structured query language (SQL). Students learn how to model business processes conceptually with entity-relationship diagrams, translate those models into normalized relational database designs, and use SQL to retrieve, transform, and analyze data for decision-making. The course progressively introduces advanced SQL techniques, including joins, aggregation, subqueries, views, and window functions, which are essential for business analytics and process analysis. Students also learn to think critically about data structure, data quality, and query logic, bridging the gap between business process understanding and data-driven decision-making.
[Master's] OPIM 5512: Data Science Using Python
  • Spring 2023, Spring 2024, Spring 2025
  • In this course, students will learn the basics of Python programming and implement advanced data science techniques based on the programming skills. With the assumption that students have concrete prior knowledge of basic data analytics but not technical familiarity with Python, this course introduces the details of Python libraries, the syntax structure and meaning, and application tips. Conceptually, it focuses on predictive analysis for real-world datasets using a wide range of methods covering linear regression, deep learning, tree-based models, and reinforcement learning.

KoreaTech

[Undergraduate] Management Database and Applications
  • Fall 2021
  • This course aims to provide a foundational understanding of database concepts and practical skills for using databases. Students will learn about the fundamental theories of database design, Structured Query Language (SQL) and how to apply these concepts through hands-on practice. The course includes designing and building databases using MS Access to simulate real-world applications. By the end of the course, students will develop basic information processing skills necessary to utilize data and databases in business contexts effectively.
